# City of Cambridge — What 30 Years of Minutes Say, in the Round

A synthesis across every built panel and published snapshot (1995–2026 corpus:
506 minutes + supporting documents). The throughline is that independent panels,
built from different tables and different methods, keep **converging on the same
few stories** — which is the test a finding has to pass to be believed.

Every insight below is graded at **Observation** or **Governance-concern**
altitude and anchored to a recognised standard (the Nolan Principles of Public
Life; the CIPFA/SOLACE good-governance principles). None asserts wrongdoing or
intent — the data shows *pattern and opportunity*, and is framed at exactly that
limit.

---

## Headline thesis

Cambridge is **not** a council with a money-corruption problem — every integrity
test on the dollars comes back null. Its story is one of **governance and
culture**: a consensus chamber that conceals real power inequality, an
accountability safeguard that decayed into a formality, and conduct that visibly
tightened under scrutiny then relaxed the moment scrutiny lifted.

> In one sentence: *Cambridge governs by consensus and ratifies its officers,
> conceals a durable power structure behind near-unanimous votes, lets
> conflict-declaration decay into formality — and behaved best only while
> someone was watching.*

---

## 1. The 2018–21 Authorised Inquiry is the hinge the whole corpus turns on

Three unrelated panels independently spike at the same event:

- **Transparency** [9]: confidential share of business ran ~2.4% for two decades,
  then jumped to a **17.2% peak in 2020** — one in six decisions taken behind
  closed doors during the Inquiry.
- **Recusal over time** [19]: stepping out of *serious* (must-leave) conflicts
  rose to **93% during the Inquiry, then collapsed to 44% afterwards**. Even on
  financial conflicts, where leaving the room is legally mandatory, it went
  **96% → 24%**.
- **Mayoral turbulence** [11]: the contested-mayoralty era (2015–23) overlaps the
  Inquiry window.

This is the City-of-Perth lesson made concrete and datable in Cambridge's own
record: **external scrutiny improves conduct; the improvement does not outlast
the scrutiny.** *(Nolan: Accountability, Openness.)*

## 2. A consensus chamber that hides a real power hierarchy

The chamber *looks* harmonious — ~76% base carry rate, the large majority of
motions unanimous, 25% of councillor-pairs statistical "allies" against 13%
opponents. Underneath:

- **Voting power** [18]: contested-vote win rates span **41% → 92%**. Dissent
  *effectiveness* decouples from dissent *volume* — the most prolific objector's
  objections fail below chance, while another councillor dissents as often and
  prevails most of the time. A 20-year councillor was outvoted two-thirds of the
  time.
- **Sponsorship network** [27/28]: in an era that was **92.8% unanimous**, who
  *seconds* whom still reveals working blocs the vote cannot — heavy mutual
  sponsors agree on **67%** of contested votes against a **54%** chamber base
  rate.

**Unanimity is the surface; the contest is real but mostly invisible.**
*(CIPFA principle B — how the chamber actually conducts its business.)*

## 3. Declaring a conflict became a ritual, not a safeguard

- **Conflict/recusal** [1]: declaring an interest raises recusal ~80×, but
  three-quarters of the time the councillor **stays and votes anyway** — and
  leans *toward* letting the matter through.
- **Recusal trend** [19]: after the Inquiry, "impartiality" declarations
  exploded to **528 declared votes at 0.4% recusal** — near-meaningless
  boilerplate. Councillors declare *more* and recuse *less*.

The identify–disclose–**manage** chain is breaking at the *manage* limb.
*(Nolan: Integrity, Objectivity; CIPFA principle A.)*

## 4. Entrenchment is structural and self-renewing

- **Tenure** [8]: median service **9.5 years**; 18 of 46 long-servers served 15+;
  Gary Mack **30.8 years**.
- **Sponsorship history** [28]: a 2000s "old guard" sponsorship clique
  **recruited the very councillors who then dominated the next 20 years** before
  fragmenting in 2008. The establishment reproduces itself.

## 5. The administration, not the chamber, is where decisions are really made

- **Officer divergence**: council ratifies the officer recommendation **97% of
  the time** (6 departures across 203 matched items). The visible debate is
  largely theatre — the substantive decision is upstream, in who *writes* the
  recommendation. This is the single most important structural caveat on
  everything else.

## 6. Residents only move outcomes in numbers

- **Objection dose–response** [12]: a lone objector is statistical noise
  (18.8% → 21.9% refusal), but **5+ coordinated objectors double the refusal rate
  to 50%**. The *act* of objecting barely matters; coordinated numbers do.
  *(CIPFA principle B — meaningful stakeholder engagement.)*

## 7. The money: concentrated and opaque, but not captured

- **Tender concentration** [2]: **$147.9M** over 351 awards; the top-10 firms
  take **48%**; **a third of the dollars ($47.8M) are redacted** behind
  "Respondent N" placeholders.
- Yet the integrity tests are all **null**: no threshold-gaming [26], no
  entrenched incumbent [15], no big-dollar planning leniency [14], no
  repeat-applicant advantage [20].

So the finding is **concentration + redaction (a transparency issue), not
detectable procurement capture.** *(CIPFA principles F/G.)*

## What the data deliberately does *not* claim — the honesty layer

The nulls are load-bearing. The **absence** of financial-corruption signatures is
itself a finding: on three independent integrity tests, Cambridge's tender record
comes back clean. Everything above sits at **Observation / Governance-concern**
altitude — patterns that *warrant explanation*, anchored to named principles,
never an assertion of motive. The one genuinely red-flag-adjacent thread (the
post-Inquiry collapse of recusal) is real, but lawful on its face.

Documented honest nulls include: deputations → dissent, December tender
"dumping", a freshman effect, big-dollar DA leniency, election-cycle
positioning, "deferred = soft no", repeat-submitter and repeat-applicant
advantage, dollar-weighted confidential spend, and procurement threshold-gaming.
See `INVESTIGATIONS.md` for the full notebook.
