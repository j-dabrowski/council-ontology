  Decision rule — dedicated schema field vs other_items catch-all:
    Do NOT default to other_items to avoid schema changes.

    Content type appears in >10% of corpus (53+ docs):
      → MUST have a dedicated typed Pydantic model + list field on ExtractedMeeting.
    Content type appears in 2–10% of corpus (10–53 docs):

  Known cross-corpus patterns (accumulated in DATA_ENRICHMENT.md from prior
  corpora and investigations) — check each of these against the rare section
  headings and other_content residual above BEFORE finalising schema decisions.
  A pattern that matches this corpus's content is a candidate for a dedicated
  field even if it wouldn't otherwise clear the >10%/2% thresholds above, since
  it has already been validated as analytically valuable on another corpus:

    [1] Structured financial-report line items (reserves / investments / transfers)
        Councils routinely report periodic financial/reserve-position statements in free text (investment schedules, budget-review reports) that are structurally repeated meeting to meeting but never captured as a typed time series — blocking any trend analysis of financial resilience even though the raw figures are demonstrably present.

    [2] Tender competitive-field metadata
        Procurement records typically capture the award value and awardee but omit the competitive-field context (bidder count, procurement method) — exactly the metadata an integrity-genre analysis needs, and exactly what redaction/summarisation drops first when a record goes confidential.

    [3] Officer recommendation captured on MINUTES motions (not just agendas)
        The officer's recommendation is conventionally restated immediately before a council's resolution text, but extraction schemas built agenda-first often only capture it from agenda documents (thinner, later-era coverage) rather than the fuller minutes record — an extraction-*source* blind spot, not a data-absence one.

    [4] Meeting-unique interest-declaration ↔ motion linkage
        Item/agenda reference numbers are commonly reused across meetings (annual or termly numbering resets), so any linkage keyed on item reference alone silently cross-contaminates unless explicitly scoped to the meeting — a generic key-design trap for any minutes-derived schema, not a Cambridge idiosyncrasy.

    [5] Full individual-vote roster (reconcile tally vs named votes)
        Minutes conventionally name only the dissenting side of a vote and state a bare tally for the rest, so any per-member analysis built on "who is recorded as voting" will systematically under-count the majority side unless the roster-vs-tally distinction is modelled explicitly — a generic minutes-drafting-convention trap.

    [6] Confidential-item REASON code (the cited closing ground)
        Confidentiality/closed-session status is commonly modelled as a bare boolean, discarding the specific statutory ground cited — this loses the ability to test whether closure grounds are applied consistently or used as a catch-all, in any jurisdiction with a ground-enumerated closed-meeting statute.

    [7] Senior-officer / CEO tenure & events timeline
        Leadership appointment/departure/acting-arrangement events typically appear in minutes as one-off motions rather than a modelled timeline entity, so leadership-churn-as-early-warning-signal (the Golden Triangle genre, Part 1.4) is invisible by default in any minutes-derived schema that doesn't deliberately model it.

    [8] Deputation / petition → item → outcome linkage
        Public-input records (deputations, petitions) are typically captured as standalone entities without a link to the specific decision they addressed, so "did public input change the outcome" can't be tested without fuzzy matching — a generic gap wherever public-input entities are extracted independently of the motions they concern.

    [9] Revenue composition over time (rates / fees / commercial / grants)
        Budget/financial entities often capture line items without a clean typed revenue-source dimension, blocking any "over-reliance on one income stream" early-warning analysis — a recognised financial-resilience indicator (Part 1.5) in general, not something specific to this corpus.

    [10] Planning application lodgement & decision dates
        A minutes-only corpus records the decision date but not necessarily the lodgement date, because minutes record the decision night, not the application's paper trail — a generic "field that looks missing but may be structurally absent from the source, not just unextracted" trap worth checking on a sample before committing re-extraction effort, on any corpus.

    [11] Savings-target delivery tracking (budget line persistence across years)
        Budget/savings-target lines are typically approved and recorded per-cycle without a persistent identifier linking the same named target across budget years, so "was this savings line ever actually delivered, or does it just keep reappearing" (Part 3.6) can't be tested without fuzzy text-matching across separate annual budget records — a generic gap wherever budget line items are extracted year-by-year without a cross-year delivery-status field.

    [12] Capital-receipt vs. revenue-line classification on disposal proceeds
        Minutes recording an asset disposal and minutes recording how its proceeds were applied are typically captured as separate free-text items without a link tagging whether the receipt was applied to capital or revenue/operating spend, so "is this council using disposal proceeds to cover recurring costs" (Part 3.7) can't be tested without manually cross-referencing two unrelated agenda items — a generic gap wherever disposal and budget-application records are extracted independently.

    [13] Severance/exit-payment amount and approval-level linkage
        Minutes recording a senior officer's departure (already targeted by #7's `senior_officer_events` entity) typically don't capture the *payment* terms of that departure as a linked, typed field — amount, approval body, and whether it exceeded ordinary contractual entitlement — so "was this exit payment properly authorised and disclosed" (Part 3.8) can't be tested even once #7's timeline entity exists, without an additional field connecting the event to its financial terms.

    [14] Statement-of-Accounts audit-opinion and timeliness tracking
        Minutes recording the audit/governance committee accepting the annual Statement of Accounts are typically captured (if at all) as an undifferentiated agenda item, without a typed field for the auditor's opinion (unqualified/qualified/disclaimed) or the gap between the statutory deadline and the actual presentation date — so "can this period's financial data be treated as audited" (Part 3.9) can't be tested as a standing check across a corpus, only noticed ad hoc if a session happens to read the relevant minute in full.

    [15] Councillor ↔ external-entity relationship graph
        Any minutes-only corpus can see *declared* interests but never the *absence* of a declaration where an undisclosed relationship existed — the highest-value integrity signal structurally requires an external join, in any jurisdiction, because the thing being tested is precisely what the primary source wouldn't record.

    [16] Cross-council comparison corpus
        Every finding on a single-council corpus carries an unanswered "is this normal?" — base rates only exist once a second/third council runs through the same pipeline. This isn't a per-council gap at all; it's the structural cost of n=1, and it resolves automatically as more councils are processed rather than needing its own re-extraction.
